polars, duckdb, linopy — how each one scales

Build a model and hand it to HiGHS, with run() never called. Every point is one process, best of two, on an idle machine. The parity gate passed on all six models: the three engines agree on the objective to 0.0e+00 relative.

Is the ordering robust to model shape?

Each engine against linopy on the same model and rung, so linopy is the flat 1.0 line. The line is the median across the six benchmark models; the band is the full min–max envelope. Below 1.0 is faster (left) or lighter (right). Where the bands do not overlap, the ordering does not depend on which model you picked.

On time the answer is robust. The polars band sits below linopy at every rung of every model bar one, and below duckdb's band outright through xs, s and m. Only at l do the two touch — 1.26 against 1.21 — and the same model, profiled, is the extreme of both, so that is one model's worst case meeting another's, not the ordering changing. duckdb crosses above linopy from m upward.

On memory it is not. Measured against linopy the two bands overlap at every rung, so neither engine is decisively lighter by that reference. Compare the engines to each other and it resolves: at l, duckdb is lighter on all six models, by 1.28x (transport) to 1.63x (fleet). That is the gap the choice actually costs — and the wide polars band is the reason it needs saying as a range rather than a number.

Past the ladder — dispatch out to 120M variables

Log–log, so a straight line is a power law and its slope is the exponent. The 40M and 120M rungs are past anything the ladder covers. duckdb is drawn twice, at its 1 GB default and unbounded — the two lie on top of each other on time, which answers whether the budget was costing it speed.

At 120M variables polars builds and loads in 35 s against linopy's 106 s and duckdb's 75 s, and the gap widens with size rather than closing: linopy's curve steepens above 10M, duckdb's above 40M, polars' does not. The right panel is where duckdb earns its keep — at 120M it holds 2.1 GB against polars' 8.3 GB, and that 2.1 GB is a number you set rather than one you measure. Unbounded, the same run takes 5.3 GB: the budget is doing real work, and it is not costing time.
