# Roadmap

What we build toward and what we have decided never to build. **This page is the
index; the issues carry the argument.** Everything is judged against the ceiling
in [docs/design/ceiling.md](design/ceiling.md#two-tiers-and-the-ceiling):
affine ∩ relational ∩ local.

## How we measure

Not "can we say what other tools say?" — that has no finite answer — but "what
is sayable in a language that must stay streamable?" A primitive is done when
`lowering.py` accepts it and the differential test passes.

Coverage against Calliope's math and PyPSA's `optimization/constraints.py` is
generated by a test rather than by hand, scoring every item **expressible**,
**out of scope by design** ([SPEC §11](SPEC.md#11-out-of-scope)) or **genuinely
arbitrary Python**. No numbers are published until
[#27](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/27) produces them.

Expressiveness is not the axis we lead on. Cost is, on the one claim a reader
can check: **end-to-end cost to a loaded solver**, wall and peak, against the
eager lane's own best path to the same place ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md)).
Build memory alone is not that claim — it is a small fraction of what solving
costs. Two claims we would rather lead on are not measured and so not made; both
are listed under [Not measured yet](benchmarks.md#not-measured-yet).

## The degree axis

Of the three admissibility rules, degree is the only *scope choice* — relational
and local fall out of the architecture. **The plan is affine-by-design**, a
decision with a revisit condition rather than an axiom. It holds because demand
is almost entirely quadratic *objectives*, and for convex 1-D curves we ship the
substitutes practitioners prefer (`piecewise: {convex: true}`, the epigraph
pattern) which keep the LP duals, warm starts and MILP compatibility a quadratic
objective gives up.

The cost side is settled and small, and the oracle is free — linopy's
`QuadraticExpression` builds the comparison. **The blocker is where it can
land**: HiGHS returns `kError` for `Hessian + integrality`, and `binary:`,
`integer:` and nonconvex `piecewise:` all ship today, so on the default path
quadratic conflicts with features already in the language.

That makes two things prerequisites rather than quadratic work — Track 4
declaring the conjunction exclusion, and a second solver on `solver_direct`
without it (Gurobi, [#106](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/106)).
Landing the primitive before either ships math the default solver refuses.

An escape cannot lift degree either way: islands return affine COO rows. The
live argument — including whether *coordinate-aligned* is the right restriction
or whether the real axis is **is the coupling declared as data?** — is
[#261](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/261) and
[#84](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/84), which supersede this
section on everything except the sequencing above.

## Track 1 — language primitives (taxed)

| # | Primitive | Proposed surface | Unlocks | Locality | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indexed access, constant | `at(x, over=dim, index=value)` | boundary conditions, multi-period linking, carrier ratios | pointwise | planned |
| 2 | Indexed access, lookup | `at(x, over=dim, index=map)` | DC power flow, hydro cascades — topology beyond nodal balance | pointwise | planned |
| 3 | Coordinate helpers in `where` | `index(dim, i)`, negative `i` from the end | "first/last snapshot" without hardcoding a label | coordinate-space | blocks 4 |
| 4 | Window sum | `sum_next_n(x, over=dim, n=N)` | rolling horizons, DSR, reserves, min up/down time | bounded-halo | needs 3 |
| 5 | Component-aware `where` | `defined(v)` over variables | masks compose across components instead of drifting | pointwise | needs design |
| 6 | `all`-reduction in `where` | `all(x, over=dim)` | reducing a mask over an out-of-`foreach` dim at all — today a `DimensionError`, since an implicit reduction would silently widen the mask | pointwise | small |
| 7 | Bounds as expressions | — | signs, bidirectional flows, component libraries | pointwise | [#31](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/31) |
| 8 | Namespacing / qualified names | — | component libraries | n/a | [#29](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/29) |
| 9 | Compose-then-build (schema merge) | — | one lower/stream pass over merged libraries | n/a | [#30](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/30) |
| 10 | Cumulative sum, data only | `cumsum(p, over=dim)` over a variable-free expression | investment schedules, cumulative budgets — today a data-prep round trip for one column | coordinate-space | [#384](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/384) |
| 11 | Semi-continuous variables | `semi_continuous: true` beside `binary`/`integer` | "off, or between min-stable and max" without the big-M pair | n/a | [#383](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/383) |

Items 1–4 are one piece of work — indexed access plus the masks that make it
usable — and are the difference between "dispatch and balances" and "network and
multi-period math". Item 2 is the adjoint of `group_sum`: same mapping table,
join without the aggregate. Calliope spells 1 and 2 as `x[dim=value]`; we do
not, because a dimension in a *kwarg key* cannot be parameterised by a macro.

**The window family is three questions.** `diff` is
`x - shift(x, over=d, by=1)` and needs nothing — it is a macro today.
Fixed-width windows are item 4, bounded-halo, the same class as `shift`.
`cumsum` splits on what it reduces: over a variable-free expression it is one
data column (item 10, free); over a **variable** row *t* carries *t* terms, so
an 8760-snapshot horizon emits ~38M nonzeros where the state-variable recurrence
emits ~26k.

That last cost is **not ours** — build throughput is ours to fix and must never
gate what the language can say, but an inflated *emitted* model is paid by the
solver on every solve. So it is priced rather than refused, and the mechanism is
[#380](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/380): whether #38's label budget
generalises from escape islands to any superlinear construct. Deciding it also
decides how much job is left for an island.

Item 11 is the cheapest thing here and blocked by nothing: HiGHS carries
`kSemiContinuous` natively (so no Track 4 finding is needed), linopy has
`Model.semi_continuous` (so the oracle is free), and it is a declaration flag
rather than a plan node. The work is the LP-file bound section, which
[ARCHITECTURE](ARCHITECTURE.md#the-relational-lane) already anticipates as a
threshold on the `cols` stream.

## Tracks 2–3 — untaxed

Nothing here touches the build path or either lane's semantics, with one flagged
exception under 2b.

- **2 — post-solve stage.** Declared derived results (LCOE, curtailment,
  emissions by group) are SQL over solution tables we already materialise.
  Sequenced after Track 1 items 1–4.
- **2b — the operational surface**, and the work that decides whether the engine
  is usable at 3am. **Dual read-back has shipped**; reduced costs and slacks ride
  the same join and have not. The headline item is **elastic relaxation**
  ([#80](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/80)) — HiGHS has no IIS, so
  slack variables with penalty costs plus a query of nonzero slacks grouped by
  block points at *which* constraints conflict, needs no solver feature and works
  on every sink. It is this track's exception: new variables mean a schema-level
  expansion pass, so it is taxed like a primitive.

  **The REPL gap is verbs, not structure.** A built model is four polars frames,
  which answers "what is in row 12" with a filter where a labelled `Dataset`
  needs a scatter — `build()` already returns the live executor, it just has
  nothing readable on it. Every verb worth having is a query over frames that
  exist: render a **bound** row (`typeset/` renders the declaration), preview
  which rows survive a `where`, evaluate an expression against bound data,
  coefficient ranges and model statistics. All of it **read-only** — inspecting a
  built model, never declaring one — which is the line that keeps this cheap and
  keeps rule 6 intact.
- **2c — value-only re-solve.** In scope because `var_label` *is* the solver
  column index with no remapping: a bound or RHS change is a label query plus
  `changeColsBounds`/`changeRowsBounds`. The precondition is that "value-only" is
  a property of the *model*, not the user's intent — a parameter read by a
  `where` changes which rows exist and shifts every later label, and a changed
  `group_sum` mapping rewrites `A`. Both are decidable off the resolved AST, and
  that check ships with the feature on the solver session
  ([#204](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/204)).

  **Integrality is label-preserving too** — it does not decide which rows exist,
  so `changeColsIntegrality` is as safe as `changeColsBounds`, and the direct
  sink already makes that call. Relax-and-fix and LP bounds on a MILP are *how
  you solve*, not what the model says. Structural editing stays out; **warm
  starts** ([#382](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/382)) are what make
  the accepted alternative — rebuild — cheap rather than merely correct.
- **2d — decomposition.** Benders and successive substitution are the case 2c is
  for, and the shape favours us, which is worth stating because the opposite is
  the natural assumption. Decomposition wants sparse triplets plus label tables;
  that **is** the model here, so each step is a query rather than a scatter — the
  master/sub split is a `GROUP BY` over `A`
  ([#39](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/39)), cut coefficients are
  `duals ⋈ subproblem rows`, and a cut in master variables lands with zero
  translation. **Appending costs nothing structurally**: adding rows moves no
  column label and renumbers no existing row, so `primal` and `dual` keep working
  across cuts — removal and reindexing are what the label contract refuses, and
  decomposition needs neither. What is missing is #204, an `addRows` on it (the
  call the sink already makes), and #39. The open question is not feasibility but
  **who writes the cut**
  ([#381](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/381)).
- **3 — AST consumers.** The first **has shipped**: `to_latex` / `to_typst` /
  `to_markdown` render a resolved model in one tree walk with no data and no
  solver, which is the claim the narrow waist was making. Remaining: CLI
  ([#35](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/35)), observability
  ([#34](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/34)), typeset follow-ups
  ([#126](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/126),
  [#321](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/321)).

## Track 4 — sink capabilities

**The ceiling and sink capability are two axes, not one.** The closure is about
streamability and is solver-independent; what a sink can *ingest* is separate.
Treating them as one let HiGHS's limits read as architectural law — the measured
table is in [benchmarks](benchmarks.md#sink-capabilities).

Adopted: a declared capability per **sink** (not per solver — `lp_file` is not a
solver but has capabilities), modelled on linopy's `Solver.features` with two
divergences. Entries are three-valued (`native` / `reformulated` / `absent`) so
satisfying one by reformulation is additive later; and the model expresses
**conjunction exclusions**, because linopy declares HiGHS with
`INTEGER_VARIABLES` *and* `QUADRATIC_OBJECTIVE` in one flat `frozenset` while
HiGHS refuses the pair. `check(model, sink=...)` takes the sink optionally: bare
`check` asks whether the model is sayable, the argument asks whether that sink
will take it. Hard rule 3 is untouched — naming an alternative sink is not the
lane redirection it forbids.

Design and work breakdown: [#89](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/89).
First spike is SOS2 on `lp_file` — no new dependency, no license question.

## Track 5 — the memory axis

The engine holds the model it builds, so peak tracks the model rather than a
number the caller sets. That is the right default and it is what makes the
lifetime disappear from the API, but two things stay open:

- **A declared ceiling.** No way today to say "build this within N gigabytes or
  fail". The honest version is partition-wise execution — slices of the leading
  `foreach` dim, sunk one at a time — which the locality closure already
  guarantees is safe. Wanted when a model is run on a machine chosen by someone
  else.
- **The solver is the larger term anyway**, by roughly an order of magnitude at
  10⁷ variables ([benchmarks](benchmarks.md)) — so a build ceiling is worth
  having for the write path and much less when the same process goes on to solve.

## Deliberate non-primitives

**Billed vs banned**: an `escape:` island
([#38](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/38)) returns affine COO rows, so
it buys back what *relational* and *local* exclude, priced against a label budget
enforced before any Python runs. It cannot buy back **degree**. Reach for one
only after #38's decision tree: different values → a parameter; different
in-language shape → a second constraint with `where`; only unsayable shapes
escape.

| Request | Why not | Instead | Escape? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadratic / bilinear terms | degree pinned at 1 | `piecewise:` (`convex: true`), or the epigraph pattern | **banned today** — blocked by the QP∧MILP exclusion on the default solver, and the bigger diff of the two |
| SOS / indicator ([#23](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/23)) | the default solver has no such concept | `piecewise:` λ-formulation; big-M for indicator, given bounds | **banned today**, and the *easier* one: `lp_file` carries it as text and Gurobi natively — needs Track 4, not a new invariant |
| Running sum over a **variable** | not locality — the *emitted* model is O(T²) rows | state-variable recurrence (a storage SOC balance *is* the rewrite) | priced, not banned — [#380](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/380) |
| Normalisations (`x / sum(x)`) | degree, not locality — a variable divisor is refused by law 9 | fix the denominator as a parameter, or state the ratio as a constraint | billed |
| Conditionals, iteration, data-dependent structure | destroys the closed AST | `where` masks + `foreach` dims; computation → data prep | billed — inside an island only |
| Resampling, clustering, IO, units | data layer, not math | preprocess; pass a parameter | n/a — free in Python already |
| Domain helpers (`reduce_carrier_dim`) | encodes one domain into the language | component libraries over generic primitives | n/a |
| Arbitrary array ops (`merge`, `reindex`) | unbounded; xarray with extra steps | data prep | billed |

## Honest snapshot

**Cheaper here, because the model is tables:** model statistics and
coefficient-range diagnostics; IIS read-back (a join, not a scatter — as dual
read-back, now shipped, already was); serialization (parquet, more portable than
netCDF); elastic relaxation; and dualization, since transposing a COO matrix is
swapping two column names.

**Ahead of comparable declarative layers:** sparse-by-construction build with no
dense intermediate, and a hand-off straight to the solver rather than through a
file; parameterised `macros:` (Calliope's sub-expressions take no arguments);
binary and integer variables; piecewise as N links with per-link signs, convex
mode and `active` gating; non-cyclic `shift`; load-time validation of every
expression, `where` string and *uncalled* macro template.

**Behind:** Track 1 items 1–6 and 10–11, plus Tracks 2 and 3. Where linopy stays
ahead it is not expressiveness but the post-solve object (labelled DataArrays vs
tidy tables — `to_dataarray` bridges), debugging (IIS via Gurobi, `print()` of a
row, against our status code), lifecycle (mutate, re-solve, warm start,
`relax`/`fix`, callbacks vs build-and-solve), solver breadth (ten backends and
four handoffs vs HiGHS-direct plus LP files,
[#106](https://github.com/FBumann/lpspec/issues/106)), and `semi_continuous`.
None are ceiling questions; all are cheaper here once written, which is why they
need scheduling rather than assuming they follow from the architecture.

**The ranking this page implies:** indexed access (items 1–4) blocks whole model
classes today; the Track 2b verbs block using the engine at 3am; solver breadth
blocks arrival from linopy at all; items 10–11 are cheap, unblocked and
unscheduled.

**Two durable losses, not scheduling.** Structure that needs the solver's
*answer* to decide the next row inside one plan, and imperative modeling at all
(rule 6). Both are the price of the closed AST — which is what buys load-time
validation, two lanes on one language, and a build that streams. Everything else
on this page is scheduling.

**Solvers we actually speak:** HiGHS through `solver_direct`, Gurobi planned on
the same path, LP files for everything else. Stated because anyone arriving from
linopy will assume solver-agnosticism; the LP route costs the float→text→parse
round trip `solver_direct` exists to remove, so "fast" is per-solver binding work
rather than a property of the engine.
