# Roadmap

What we are building toward, how we know when a piece is done, and what we
have deliberately decided never to build. Ordered work lives in GitHub
issues; this file is the index and the rationale.

## How we measure capability

Two questions get confused: *"can we say what other tools say?"* and *"what
is sayable at all in a language that must stay streamable?"* Only the second
has a finite answer, so it is the one we specify against.

**The ceiling (what we build toward).** A candidate primitive enters the
language iff it is:

1. **degree 1 (affine)** — `variable × parameter` only, never
   `variable × variable` (see [The degree axis](#the-degree-axis));
2. **relational** — expressible as filter / join / group-by-aggregate over
   tidy tables; and
3. **local** — *pointwise* or *bounded-halo* in the sense of
   [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#the-expressive-ceiling). Global
   operators are rejected at lowering with a rewrite hint.

This is provable rather than aspirational: the done-condition for a
primitive is that `lowering.py` accepts it and the differential test against
the linopy oracle passes. It is also *finite* — the list below is close to
complete, not a wish list that grows with every request.

**The score (what we publish).** Coverage against two external corpora,
generated by a test rather than written by hand (#27):

| Corpus | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Calliope base math + published custom-math library | can we express a decade of real energy-system math? |
| PyPSA `optimization/constraints.py` | what share of hand-written linopy math becomes YAML? (use case #2) |

Every corpus item scores as **(a) expressible**, **(b) out of scope by
design** (data prep, see [SPEC §10](SPEC.md#10-out-of-scope)), or **(c)
genuinely arbitrary Python** (an `escape:` island, or stays a callback). The
(a)/(b)/(c) split is the point: it converts an unbounded comparison into a
measured number with a principled remainder.

*No coverage numbers are published here until the harness in #27 produces
them.*

### Provisional operator-level scoring

Hand-written, and it scores *vocabulary* rather than math: one row per
construct the Calliope corpus exposes as a named operator or key. It does
not pre-empt #27 and must not be quoted as coverage — operator coverage
answers "do we have the words?", the harness answers "can we say the
sentences?", and the second is the number we publish. It also flatters us
by construction: math their users write out by hand, with no helper and no
keyword, never becomes a row.

Its use is triage. The relational-shape column is the admissibility test
from [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#the-expressive-ceiling) applied
mechanically, so a verdict is derived rather than argued.

| Their construct | Ours | Relational shape | Locality | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sum(x, over=d)` | `sum` | drop a dim column | pointwise | **have** |
| `group_sum(x, m, d)` | `group_sum` | equi-join a mapping | pointwise | **have** |
| `roll(x, d=n)` | `roll` | self-join on `ord`, modulo | bounded-halo | **have** |
| `select_from_lookup_arrays` | — | the `group_sum` join, no aggregate | pointwise | **(a)** — Track 1 item 2 |
| `get_val_at_index(d=i)` | — | dim table only | coordinate-space | **(a)** — Track 1 item 3 |
| `sum_next_n(x, d, N)` | — | range join on `ord` | bounded-halo | **(a)** — Track 1 item 4 |
| `any(param, over=d)` in `where` | implicit | `bool_or` aggregate | pointwise | **have** (as the default reduction); `all` is item 6 |
| `group_datetime(x, t, period)` | — | `group_sum` along a derived mapping | pointwise | **(b)** — compute the mapping in data prep, then `group_sum`. No primitive |
| `x ** y` | refused | — | — | **(b)/(c)** — degree 1 with a variable base; data prep otherwise |
| `order:` on expressions | — | — | — | **unnecessary by construction** — our `expressions:` substitute, so no evaluation order exists to declare |
| `where(x, cond)` in an expression | — | ? | ? | **unjudged** — may reduce to equation-level `where`, may be a real gap |

Two of those verdicts are ones a prose comparison does not produce:

- **unnecessary by construction** — distinct from missing; the concept has
  nowhere to attach in our design, so no amount of scope would add it;
- **unjudged** — a live cell, kept visible in the table rather than
  resolved by omission.

**The second axis.** Expressiveness is not the axis we lead on. Scale —
declarative math at 10⁸ variables under a fixed memory budget — is
unmatched and is measured separately ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md),
`docs/bench.svg`). Language work must not crowd out that number.

**Explicit non-yardsticks.** Feature parity with Calliope's math language
(a lower bound found empirically, not a target — see *Deliberate
non-primitives*) and operation parity with xarray/pandas (unbounded, and
hostile to the closed-AST property that makes streaming possible).

## The degree axis

**Linear stays the goal.** Of the three admissibility rules, degree is the
only one that is a *scope choice* rather than a consequence of streaming —
relational and local fall out of the architecture, degree 1 does not. So it
is recorded here as a decision with a revisit condition, not as an axiom.

**The decision** (SPEC §12.4, 2026-07-24): the IR is affine-by-design,
quadratic out of scope. Note the premise has since narrowed: that call was
made when the relational lane was an optimization lane with automatic
fallback to a feature-complete eager builder, so "affine-only" was a routing
decision. Hard rule 3 removed the fallback, which makes it a product
ceiling. The conclusion still holds — the reasons below are about cost and
substitutes, not about routing — but it is now load-bearing and should be
re-derived, not inherited.

**Why it holds today.**

- The demand is almost entirely *quadratic objectives* (fuel cost curves,
  ohmic losses, risk and reserve terms, proximal terms under decomposition).
  Quadratic *constraints* are a separate problem class that HiGHS cannot
  solve at all and that breaks the `(row, col, coeff)` sink model far more
  deeply than a Hessian does.
- For convex 1-D cost curves we already ship the substitutes practitioners
  prefer: `piecewise:` with `convex: true` (pure-LP hull, no binaries) and
  the epigraph pattern in plain YAML (`tests/test_piecewise_convex.py`).
  These keep LP duals, warm starts, and MILP compatibility — a quadratic
  objective gives those up.
- Every sink assumes affine rows. Degree 2 means a second stream in each
  (highspy `passHessian`, LP/MPS quadratic sections), a data-time convexity
  guard, and differential coverage of a new problem class.

**What would move it.** All four, not any one — the live argument over how close
each is to met lives in [#84](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/84),
not here:

1. A model where the *approximation* is the problem, not the formulation —
   in practice that means **bilinear terms across dimensions**, where PWL is
   exponential in breakpoints. A 1-D convex curve is not evidence.
2. Sinks carry a quadratic stream.
3. A data-time convexity guard exists (precedent: the piecewise curvature
   guard).
4. The oracle covers it — linopy's `QuadraticExpression` does, so
   differential testing stays possible.

**An escape does not lift this one.** `escape:` islands ([#38](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/38)) return affine COO
rows into the same sink, and no sink carries a quadratic stream — so unlike
the global and non-relational exclusions, degree ≥ 2 cannot be bought with a
budget. That is independent confirmation of the pin: condition 2 below (a
quadratic stream in the sinks) is load-bearing for *any* route to degree 2,
not just the language route.

**Scope if it ever lands**, fixed in advance so the discussion cannot drift:
**degree ≤ 2, factors coordinate-aligned, objective only, convex-guarded.**
Aligned products (`p * p` at the same coordinates) are a pointwise
self-join — one more column on the term table, and streaming is unaffected.
General bilinear coupling over arbitrary pairs is a cross join, |terms|²
rows, and violates the primary memory invariant; it is excluded permanently,
not pending.

## Track 1 — language primitives (taxed)

Each row costs the full two-backend tax in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#extension-checklists): eager helper, IR
node + locality class, executor case, lowering case, differential tests,
SPEC entry.

| # | Primitive | Proposed surface | Unlocks | Locality | Lowering | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Indexed access, constant** | `at(x, over=dim, index=value)` | terminal/boundary conditions, multi-period investment linking, carrier ratios | pointwise | equality filter on a dim column | planned — SPEC Q3 |
| 2 | **Indexed access, lookup (gather)** | `at(x, over=dim, index=map)` | DC power flow, hydro cascades, parent/child tech linking — all topology math beyond nodal balance | pointwise | the `group_sum` join without the `GROUP BY` | planned |
| 3 | **Coordinate helpers in `where`** | `index(dim, i)`, negative `i` for from-end | "first/last snapshot" without hardcoding a label; boundary masks on datetime dims | coordinate-space | dim table only — never touches data | planned; blocks 4 |
| 4 | **Window sum** | `sum_next_n(x, over=dim, n=N)` | rolling-horizon limits, demand-side response, reserve horizons, min up/down time | bounded-halo | range join, same class as `roll`/`shift` | planned; needs 3 for its boundary mask |
| 5 | **Component-aware `where`** | `defined(v)` over variables/expressions | masks compose across components instead of being re-derived from parameters at each use site (and silently drifting) | pointwise | existence semi-join on the component's table | needs design |
| 6 | **`all`-reduction in `where`** | `all(x, over=dim)` | today an out-of-`foreach` mask dim is always reduced with `any`; `all` is unreachable | pointwise | `bool_and` aggregate | small |
| 7 | Bounds as expressions | — | signs, bidirectional flows, component libraries | pointwise | — | #31 |
| 8 | Namespacing / qualified names | — | component libraries | n/a | n/a | #29 |
| 9 | Compose-then-build (schema merge) | — | one lower/stream pass over merged libraries | n/a | n/a | #30 |

Items 1–4 are one coherent piece of work — **indexed access plus the masks
that make it usable** — and are the difference between "dispatch and
balances" and "network and multi-period math".

**Surface-syntax note.** Calliope spells 1 and 2 as `x[dim=value]` /
`x[dim=$lookup]`. We deliberately do not: a dimension in a *kwarg key*
cannot be parameterised by a macro — the same trap as the `roll(x,
snapshot=1)` design that ARCHITECTURE names as its own counterexample. Both
indexed-access forms keep the dim in a **value** position, so macros can
abstract over them.

## Track 2 — post-solve stage (new)

We have no post-solve expression layer at all: solutions come back as tables
and every derived quantity is Python the user writes. Declared derived
results (LCOE, curtailment, emissions by group — Calliope's
`postprocessed`) are SQL over solution tables we already materialise, and
carry **no tax on the build path**. Sequenced after Track 1 items 1–4.

## Track 2b — the operational surface

Items 1–4 are queries over tables we already materialise, so none of them
touch the build path or either lane's semantics. The track is listed because
"cheap once written" and "will exist" are different claims, and this is the
work that decides whether the engine is usable at 3am.

1. **Dual read-back** — a join, same shape as `primal`; scheduled as
   [#78](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/78). `getSolution()`
   is already called and `row_dual` simply never fetched.
2. **IIS read-back**, where a solver does provide one: row indices join to
   the constraint label tables, which is tidier than linopy's scatter. HiGHS
   has none, so this arrives with the Gurobi direct sink
   ([#28](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/28)) — the read-back
   is the easy half.
3. **Algebraic rendering of a row.** linopy's `print()` of a constraint;
   here a `string_agg` over `A` joined to the label tables — roughly one
   query.
4. **Model statistics and coefficient-range diagnostics** — queries over
   `cols` / `rows` / `A`.
5. **Elastic relaxation** — the infeasibility answer for everyone not on
   Gurobi: slack variables with penalty costs, solve, then query the nonzero
   slacks grouped by constraint block. No solver feature, every sink, every
   solver, and it names *which* constraints conflict and by how much rather
   than a minimal set. Unlike 1–4 it is **not** a query — it needs a
   schema-level expansion pass, the way `piecewise:` does — so it sits later
   on the roadmap, behind item 1:
   [#80](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/80).

## Track 2c — value-only re-solve

Tracked as [#82](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/82). §12.2 puts
"incremental model editing" out of scope, which currently reads as covering
both structural and value-only changes. Split it:

- **In scope: value-only.** Because `var_label` *is* the solver column index
  and `row` the solver row index, with no remapping, changing a bound or an
  RHS is a label query plus `changeColsBounds` / `changeRowsBounds`. That is
  cleaner here than in linopy, not worse — and it is what rolling horizon,
  parameter sweeps and Benders subproblems actually need. With one condition:
  "value-only" has to be a property of the *model*, not of the user's
  intent — a parameter that appears in a `where` or a `group_sum` mapping
  changes row existence, so the labels move and an in-place bound update
  writes to the wrong columns. That is statically decidable off the resolved
  AST and ships with the feature (#82).
- **Out of scope: structural.** Adding or removing variables and constraints
  invalidates the label contract, and rebuilding is the supported answer.
- **Related gap: warm starts.** linopy passes basis files; `solver_direct`
  would need to set one through highspy. Without it, a sweep re-solves from
  scratch and loses most of what value-only editing buys.

## Solvers we actually speak

linopy speaks most commercial and open solvers. We do not, and anyone
arriving from linopy will assume otherwise, so: **HiGHS through
`solver_direct`, Gurobi planned on the same path, and LP files for everything
else.** The LP route costs the float→text→parse round trip that
`solver_direct` exists to remove, so "fast" is per-solver binding work rather
than a property of the engine.

## Track 3 — AST consumers (untaxed)

Consumers of the typed AST add capability without touching either backend
(#21):

- **Math → LaTeX rendering.** The product *is* the declarative math; it
  should render as typeset documentation. A tree walk, no data required.
  Supersedes the stale "planned v2" note in SPEC §10.
- CLI `check` / `solve` / `write` (#35), executor observability (#34).

## Deliberate non-primitives

Recorded with their rewrite so the same request does not get re-litigated.
**Billed vs banned**: an `escape:` island ([#38](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/38)) returns affine COO rows, so it
can buy back what the *relational* and *local* rules exclude — priced
against a label budget, enforced before any Python runs. It cannot buy back
**degree**: no sink carries a quadratic stream, so that row is banned rather
than priced. Reach for an escape only after the decision tree in #38
(different values → parameter; different in-language shape → a second
constraint with `where`; only unsayable shapes escape).

| Request | Why not | Instead | Escape? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadratic / bilinear terms | degree axis pinned at 1 — see [The degree axis](#the-degree-axis) | `piecewise:` (`convex: true` for a pure-LP hull) or the epigraph pattern | **banned today** — whether it is *reachable* is an open discussion, not a settled claim: [#84](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/84) |
| SOS / indicator constraints ([#23](https://github.com/FBumann/linopy-yaml/issues/23)) | no sink carries the stream | `piecewise:` λ-formulation (SOS2's usual purpose) | **banned** — and note which half is missing: the oracle exists (linopy has it), the sink stream does not, and adding one means a new stream in *every* sink |
| Cumulative / running sums, normalisations | **global** operator — breaks partition-wise execution | state-variable recurrence (a storage SOC balance *is* the rewrite) | billed — the rewrite is still the right answer at scale (O(T) vs O(T²)) |
| Conditionals, iteration, data-dependent structure in expressions | destroys the closed AST that makes streaming possible | `where` masks + `foreach` dims; computation moves to data prep | billed — inside an escape island only, never in the symbolic plan |
| Resampling, clustering, interpolation, IO, units | data layer, not math | preprocess; pass a parameter (SPEC §10) | n/a — free in Python already, no escape needed |
| Domain helpers (e.g. Calliope's `reduce_carrier_dim`) | encodes one domain's conventions into the language | component libraries over generic primitives | n/a |
| Arbitrary array ops (`merge`, `reindex`, `apply_ufunc`) | unbounded; would make the language xarray with extra steps | data prep | billed — inside an escape island |

## Honest snapshot

**Cheaper here than in linopy, because the model is tables:** model
statistics and coefficient-range diagnostics (queries); dual and IIS
read-back (joins against the label tables, rather than a scatter);
serialization (parquet, and more portable than netCDF); elastic relaxation
(Track 2b); and dualization — transposing a COO matrix is swapping two column
names, which is the one place linopy has to work harder than we do. Worth
stating in the docs so the tradeoff reads as a trade rather than a deficit.

**Ahead of comparable declarative layers:** memory-bounded streaming build;
parameterised `macros` (Calliope's sub-expressions take no arguments);
binary variables and MILP (Calliope's schema has `real|integer` only);
piecewise as N links with per-link signs, convex mode and `active` gating;
non-cyclic `shift`; load-time validation of every expression, where string
and *uncalled* macro template.

**Behind:** everything in Track 1 items 1–6, plus Tracks 2 and 3.
